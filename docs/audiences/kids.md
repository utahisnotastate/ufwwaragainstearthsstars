# Hello, Space Explorer! (For Kids)

> **In one sentence:** Scientists have collected so much space data that some discoveries might be hiding in it — and you can learn how to look!

*This page is for kids about 6 to 12 years old. Grown-ups can read it aloud.*

## What is this project?

Telescopes and spacecraft take pictures and measurements of space every night. That is a **lot** of data. More than people can look at closely!

Some of that data is saved for later. We think a few amazing discoveries might be hiding in it. This project explains **where to look**, **how to look**, and **how sure we are** about each idea.

## Wait — "alien planets"? Like aliens?

Good question! In astronomy, an **alien planet** just means a planet that goes around a **different star**, not our Sun. Scientists also call them **exoplanets**. There are thousands of them!

This project is **not** about aliens or spaceships. We have not found any aliens. We are looking for planets, moons, space rocks and other natural things.

## Five true facts that sound made up

1. **Three visitors from other stars have flown through our Solar System.** Their names are ʻOumuamua, Borisov and 3I/ATLAS. They came, swung past the Sun, and left forever.
2. **One of them was found by one person with a homemade telescope.** Gennady Borisov built his own telescope and found the second visitor in 2019.
3. **A planet's shadow disappeared!** A planet called TOI-2535 b used to pass in front of its star. Then its path tilted, and in 2026 it stopped passing in front. Scientists think a hidden planet is tilting it.
4. **Some planets float alone.** They do not go around any star. Scientists find them when they act like a magnifying glass for a star far behind them.
5. **Neptune was found with math first.** In 1846, scientists noticed Uranus was being pulled by something. They calculated where to look — and found Neptune!

## How do scientists know things?

Scientists are careful. They sort ideas by how sure they are. We use four labels:

| Label | What it means |
|---|---|
| 🟢 **Level 1 — Confirmed** | We know this for sure. It has been checked many times. |
| 🔵 **Level 2 — Strong evidence** | Scientists are pretty sure, but they are still checking. |
| 🟡 **Level 3 — Testable idea** | It makes sense, but nobody has proven it yet. You can help test it! |
| 🟠 **Level 4 — Long shot** | It could be true, but it is very hard to find with the tools we have. |

When you read about space online, ask: **"Which level is this?"** That is how real scientists think.

## Space safety rules

1. **Never look at the Sun.** Not with your eyes, not with sunglasses, not with binoculars, a telescope or a phone. It can hurt your eyes forever.
2. **Ask a grown-up** before using lamps, glass, magnets, pins or anything sharp or hot.
3. **Walk, do not run,** in the moving activities.
4. **Never go outside at night alone** to look at the sky. Stargaze with a grown-up.

## Can kids really help scientists?

Yes! Real projects let anyone help look at real space data. A grown-up can help you sign up. Two examples on the **Zooniverse** website:

- **Backyard Worlds: Planet 9** — look at "flip-books" of sky pictures to spot cold, dark objects moving near the Sun.
- **Planet Hunters TESS** — look at star brightness graphs to spot the dips that planets make.

Volunteers on these projects have helped find real brown dwarfs and real planets. (Zooniverse has rules about age and accounts, so a grown-up should help.)

## Words to know

- **Planet** — a big round world that goes around a star.
- **Star** — a giant ball of hot gas that makes its own light, like our Sun.
- **Orbit** — the path one thing takes around another.
- **Transit** — when a planet passes in front of its star.
- **Telescope** — a tool that collects light so we can see faint, far-away things.
- **Data** — information scientists collect, like pictures and measurements.

You can find more words in the [glossary](../about/glossary.md). Each word has a kid explanation and a grown-up explanation.
