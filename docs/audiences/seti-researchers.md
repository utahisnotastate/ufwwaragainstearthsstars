# Guide for SETI and Technosignature Researchers

> **In one sentence:** This project is about natural objects — planets, moons, brown dwarfs and small bodies — but several disclosures overlap with SETI practice (interstellar objects as targets, low-frequency radio archives, anomaly triage), and we state plainly that there is no evidence of alien technology associated with any of them.

## For young readers

SETI means the **Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence**. SETI scientists listen for radio signals or look for other signs that someone built technology on another world. So far, nobody has found any. This project is about natural things like planets and space rocks, but SETI scientists use some of the same tools. When scientists pointed radio telescopes at the three visitors from other stars, they heard only silence — the visitors look like natural comets and rocks.

## Our position

- **No claim of extraterrestrial technology** is made anywhere in this project. "Alien planets" means exoplanets.
- Interstellar objects 1I/ʻOumuamua, 2I/Borisov and 3I/ATLAS have all been observed in technosignature searches, with **no detections** (🟢 Level 1 for the null results).
- The best-supported explanations for all three are natural (🔵 Level 2 for ʻOumuamua's non-gravitational acceleration mechanism, which is still discussed; 🟢 Level 1 for the cometary nature of 2I and 3I).

## Interstellar objects as SETI targets

| Object | Technosignature observations (examples) | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1I/ʻOumuamua (2017) | Breakthrough Listen with the Green Bank Telescope (Enriquez et al. 2018); Murchison Widefield Array (Tingay et al. 2018) | No detection |
| 2I/Borisov (2019) | Breakthrough Listen with the Green Bank Telescope | No detection |
| 3I/ATLAS (2025) | Breakthrough Listen with the Green Bank Telescope at 1–12 GHz on 18 December 2025, about a day before closest approach to Earth (Jacobson-Bell et al. 2025); an Allen Telescope Array search was also reported | No credible narrowband signals; the GBT result rules out isotropic continuous-wave transmitters above roughly 0.1 W at the object |

**On ʻOumuamua.** Its small non-gravitational acceleration without visible coma prompted many hypotheses, including an artificial light sail (Bialy & Loeb 2018). Natural explanations are favored by most researchers; for example, Bergner & Seligman (2023) showed that outgassing of molecular hydrogen produced by cosmic-ray processing of water ice could explain the acceleration.

**Why this matters for [D14](../disclosures/d14-interstellar-objects-unlinked-data.md).** If archival searches find additional interstellar objects (🟡 Level 3), each is a natural target for rapid characterization, including technosignature observations when appropriate. Earlier detection — for example from unlinked survey observations — means longer observing windows.

## Radio archives ([D08](../disclosures/d08-radio-auroras.md))

D08 proposes searching low-frequency survey data for **natural** auroral emission from unknown brown dwarfs or exoplanets: electron cyclotron maser emission that is strongly circularly polarized, often bursty, and near the local cyclotron frequency. This differs from typical SETI signal models:

| Property | Natural auroral (ECMI) emission | Typical narrowband technosignature model |
|---|---|---|
| Bandwidth | Broad (MHz-scale), often with fine structure | Very narrow (Hz-scale) |
| Polarization | High circular polarization | Model-dependent |
| Time behavior | Bursty, rotation-modulated | Model-dependent; often continuous or pulsed |
| Doppler drift | Not characteristic | Drift from transmitter/receiver acceleration is a key filter |

SETI teams have long experience distinguishing radio frequency interference (RFI) from astrophysical signals using on–off cadences, drift-rate searches and multi-beam rejection. Those methods are directly useful for D08-style searches, and we would welcome advice.

## Anomaly triage: shared lessons

Several technosignature candidates have later been explained naturally or as contamination. For example, infrared-excess searches for Dyson-sphere candidates in Gaia, 2MASS and WISE data produced candidates whose follow-up pointed to contamination by background sources for at least some of them. The general lessons apply across this project:

1. **Calibrate false-alarm rates** for archive-scale searches (look-elsewhere effect).
2. **Prefer explanations within known physics** until they are ruled out.
3. **Seek independent confirmation** with different instruments.
4. **Communicate uncertainty** clearly to the public.

## Biosignatures via polarization (adjacent to [D05](../disclosures/d05-polarized-light-from-planets.md))

Circular polarization from homochiral biological pigments has been studied as a possible remote biosignature (for example, Sparks et al. 2009 measured circular polarization from photosynthetic microbes in the lab). Applying this to exoplanets requires extreme contrast and polarimetric precision beyond current capabilities (🟠 Level 4).

## How SETI researchers could contribute

- Review our language about interstellar objects and technosignatures for accuracy.
- Share RFI-rejection and drift-search methods applicable to D08 searches.
- Coordinate observing proposals if a new interstellar object is identified early.
