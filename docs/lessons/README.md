# Lessons

> **In one sentence:** Hands-on lessons that let learners from age 6 to university discover how astronomers find things they cannot see directly.

## For young readers

These lessons are like science recipes. Each one has a question, a list of things you need, and steps to follow. You will use lamps, balls, string, sunglasses and your own thumb. By the end, you will know some of the same tricks real astronomers use!

## How to use these lessons

- Every lesson can be done in a classroom, a library, a club or at home.
- Each lesson lists an **age band**. These are suggestions. Younger learners can do the activity and skip the math. Older learners can add the extension.
- Each lesson ends with a **"For the teacher"** section. That is the expert layer. It explains the real science and gives references.
- Every lesson links to the **disclosure** it connects to. A disclosure is a short report about where new discoveries might be hiding in public data. See [the disclosure registry](../disclosures/README.md).

Teachers and tutors: please also read the [guide for teachers and tutors](../audiences/teachers-and-tutors.md). It has a misconception list, differentiation tips and assessment ideas.

### About the NGSS codes

NGSS means the U.S. **Next Generation Science Standards**. The codes in the table are performance expectations that the lesson **supports**. A single lesson rarely covers a whole performance expectation. Teachers outside the United States can map the same goals to their own curriculum.

Short reminders of what each code is about:

| Code | Topic (short summary) |
|---|---|
| 1-ESS1-1 | Patterns of the Sun, Moon and stars that can be predicted |
| 4-PS4-2 | Light reflecting from objects into the eye lets us see them |
| 5-ESS1-1 | Stars look dimmer or brighter because of their distance |
| 5-PS2-1 | Earth's gravity pulls objects down |
| MS-ESS1-1 | Model of the Earth–Sun–Moon system (phases, eclipses, seasons) |
| MS-ESS1-2 | The role of gravity in motions in the Solar System and galaxies |
| MS-ESS1-3 | Scale properties of objects in the Solar System |
| MS-PS2-3 | Factors that affect the strength of electric and magnetic forces |
| MS-PS2-4 | Gravitational interactions are attractive and depend on mass |
| MS-PS4-2 | Waves are reflected, absorbed or transmitted by materials |
| HS-ESS1-4 | Math or computer models of the motion of orbiting objects |
| HS-PS2-4 | Newton's law of gravitation and Coulomb's law |

## Safety rules for every lesson

1. **Never look at the Sun** — not with your eyes, sunglasses, binoculars, a telescope or a phone camera. It can hurt your eyes forever.
2. **Lamps get hot.** Use LED lamps when you can. Do not touch bulbs.
3. **Glass can break.** Use plastic where the lesson allows. An adult should handle real glass.
4. **Pins and needles are sharp.** Younger children should use tape instead of pins.
5. **Magnets can pinch fingers.** Keep strong magnets away from phones, cards, pacemakers and small children (swallowed magnets are dangerous). Keep iron filings sealed.
6. **Walking activities:** walk, do not run. Keep clear lanes. Stop if anyone feels dizzy.
7. **Laser pointers are not needed** for any lesson. Do not use them.

## Accessibility

- Each activity has a version that does not depend on sight. For example, learners can **feel** the dip in a stretched sheet, or **hear** brightness changes using a sound-output light-meter app.
- Read-aloud versions: the "For young readers" boxes are written to be read aloud.
- Learners who use wheelchairs can take the "Sun" or "timer" role in walking activities, or the teacher can switch to a tabletop version (each lesson notes one).
