# Social Media Style Guide

> **In one sentence:** Our posts should be clear enough for a 10-year-old, careful enough for a scientist, and kind to everyone.

## For young readers

Grown-ups run this project's social media accounts. If you are a kid and want to share something you learned, ask a parent, guardian or teacher to help you. Never share your name, school, address or photos online without a trusted adult.

## 1. Voice

- **Curious, calm and kind.** We sound like a good science teacher, not a movie trailer.
- **Plain words.** About a 4th–6th grade reading level for the main text. Explain every science word the first time.
- **Short sentences.** One idea per sentence. Under about 20 words.
- **"We" and "you."** We are the project. You are the reader.
- **Respectful of scientists.** Survey teams, archive staff and researchers made all this data possible. We never say anyone "missed", "ignored" or "hid" something. We say "there is more data than people have had time to study."

## 2. Evidence labels in every science post

Every post that makes a science claim includes its evidence level **in words**, not only an emoji:

| Write it like this | Means |
|---|---|
| 🟢 Level 1 — Confirmed | Seen and checked more than once |
| 🔵 Level 2 — Strong evidence | Good published results; experts still discuss them |
| 🟡 Level 3 — Testable idea | Follows normal physics; not shown yet; you can help test it |
| 🟠 Level 4 — Long shot | Physics allows it, but today's tools may be too weak |

If a post mixes levels, label the main claim and link to the dossier for the rest.

## 3. Words we avoid

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| "proves", "confirmed" (unless independently published) | "suggests", "is evidence for", "a candidate" |
| "NASA missed this", "scientists are blind to" | "there is more data than anyone has had time to study" |
| "secret", "hidden files", "cover-up" | "public data", "archives", "not yet studied in detail" |
| "breakthrough", "game-changer", "mind-blowing" | describe what actually happened |
| "alien" (alone) | "exoplanet" or "planet around another star" |
| "100% sure", "guaranteed" | give the evidence level |

## 4. Emoji

- Use at most **two** per post.
- Use them as helpers, not as meaning. The evidence-level emoji always comes with the words.
- Good: 🪐 ☄️ 🔭 🌌 🌍 🌙 ⭐ 📡
- Avoid: 👽 🛸 (they suggest alien claims we are not making), 🤯 💥 🔥 (hype).

## 5. Images and alt text

- Every image needs **alt text** that says what the image shows and why it matters.
- Credit every image. Use only images we are allowed to use (for example, many NASA images are public domain, but always check the credit line; ESA and ESO images have their own license terms).
- Label artist's illustrations as **"Illustration"** so no one mistakes them for photos.
- Do not use color alone to carry meaning.

**Alt text pattern:** *"[Type of image]: [what it shows]. [Why it matters in one short sentence]."*
Example: *"Diagram: a closed oval orbit around the Sun next to an open curve that never comes back. The open curve is the path of an interstellar visitor."*

## 6. Things we never post

1. **Unverified "discoveries."** Possible finds go through the [flag-to-report pipeline](../plan/disclosure-plan.md#9-from-flag-to-report-what-happens-to-a-possible-discovery) first. No exceptions.
2. **Personal attacks** on anyone — scientists, agencies, critics or commenters.
3. **Claims that anyone is hiding data.**
4. **Health, wellness or "frequency" advice** of any kind.
5. **Claims about alien life or alien technology.**
6. **Children's personal information:** names, faces, schools, locations, ages with names, or anything that could identify a child. Share kids' work only with written permission from a parent or guardian, and only without identifying details.
7. **Private information** about anyone (addresses, emails, phone numbers).
8. **Images we do not have rights to use.**
9. **Unsafe activities.** Never suggest looking at the Sun without certified solar viewing filters.

## 7. The two-person rule

No science post goes out until a **second person** has checked it against the dossier. The [posting calendar](posting-calendar.md) lists who reviews each post.

## 8. How to correct a mistake in public

1. **Quote-post the original** from the same account: *"Correction: In this post we said ___. That was wrong. The correct information is ___. Thanks to ___ for pointing it out."*
2. **Fix the dossier** and add a dated correction note at the bottom.
3. **Add a line to `CHANGELOG.md`.**
4. **Do not delete** the original unless it contains private information or could cause harm. If we delete, we say so and why.
5. **Thank the person** who found it (with their permission to name them).

## 9. Replying to common questions

### "Is it aliens?"

Be friendly. Curiosity is good.

> "Great question! So far, no. Interstellar objects like 3I/ATLAS behave like natural comets and rocks from other star systems. That's still amazing — it's a piece of another solar system passing through ours. If scientists ever found real evidence of alien technology, it would need lots of careful checks by many teams. 🔭"

### "Why didn't NASA find this already?"

> "Telescopes collect far more data than anyone has time to study closely. Teams have to choose what to follow up. That's why public archives matter: anyone can help look. Every disclosure we share uses data that scientists collected and made public."

### "Is this a secret disclosure / leak?"

> "No secrets here! 'Disclosure' is just our word for a short, honest report about where discoveries might be hiding in public data. Everything we use is public and published."

### "Can I cure / heal / boost my brain with [frequency]?"

> "We only cover astronomy, and we don't give health advice. For health questions, please talk to a doctor."

### "You're wrong about X."

> "Thank you — we want to get this right. Could you share a source? We'll check it and post a correction if needed."

### A child replies directly

Reply warmly and briefly. Do not ask for personal details. Do not move to private messages. If the child shares personal details, hide or remove the reply if the platform allows, and post a gentle general reminder about online safety.

## 10. Moderation

- Hide or remove replies that harass anyone, share private information, or spread health misinformation.
- Do not argue with bad-faith accounts. One polite, factual reply, then move on.
- Block accounts that target children.
- Keep a simple moderation log (date, action, reason) without storing personal data.

## 11. Checklist before posting

- [ ] Matches the dossier exactly (numbers, names, dates).
- [ ] Evidence level written in words.
- [ ] No words from the "avoid" list.
- [ ] At most two emoji; none suggesting aliens or hype.
- [ ] Alt text on every image; illustrations labeled.
- [ ] Links point to the right page.
- [ ] Second-person science check done.
- [ ] No children's personal information.
