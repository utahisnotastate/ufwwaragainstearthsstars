# Glossary

> **In one sentence:** Every special word used in this project, explained twice — once simply, and once for experts.

## For young readers

When you see a word you don't know, look it up here. Read the **Simple** line first. The **Expert** line is for grown-ups and older students who want more detail.

---

## A

**Achromatic**
- *Simple:* The same in every color.
- *Expert:* Wavelength-independent. Gravitational microlensing magnification is achromatic for a point source, which helps distinguish it from stellar flares.

**ALMA (Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array)**
- *Simple:* A group of 66 radio dishes in a high desert in Chile that can see cold dust and gas around young stars.
- *Expert:* An interferometer operating at roughly 0.3–9 mm wavelengths, used for imaging protoplanetary disks in continuum and molecular lines.

**Aphelion / Perihelion**
- *Simple:* The farthest and closest points of an orbit to the Sun.
- *Expert:* Apsides of a heliocentric orbit; perihelion distance *q* = *a*(1 − *e*), aphelion *Q* = *a*(1 + *e*) (bound orbits only).

**Asteroid**
- *Simple:* A rocky or metal space rock that goes around the Sun.
- *Expert:* A small Solar System body, usually inactive (no coma), mostly in the main belt between Mars and Jupiter.

**Astrometry**
- *Simple:* Measuring exactly where things are in the sky.
- *Expert:* Precise measurement of celestial positions and their changes (proper motion, parallax, orbital motion).

**Astronomical unit (au)**
- *Simple:* The average distance from Earth to the Sun, about 150 million km.
- *Expert:* Defined as exactly 149,597,870,700 m (IAU 2012).

**Aurora**
- *Simple:* Glowing lights in the sky near a planet's poles, made when charged particles hit the air.
- *Expert:* Emission from atmospheric species excited by precipitating charged particles guided by magnetic fields; accompanied by coherent radio emission.

## B

**Barycenter**
- *Simple:* The balance point that two objects circle around together.
- *Expert:* The center of mass of a system; both bodies of a binary orbit it.

**Brown dwarf**
- *Simple:* An object bigger than a planet but too small to shine like a star. It glows faintly from leftover heat.
- *Expert:* A substellar object (roughly 13–80 Jupiter masses) that cannot sustain hydrogen fusion; spectral types L, T and Y.

## C

**Centaur**
- *Simple:* An icy space object that orbits between Jupiter and Neptune.
- *Expert:* A small body on an orbit crossing the giant-planet region, dynamically short-lived; many are transitioning from the Kuiper Belt.

**Citizen science**
- *Simple:* Science that anyone can help with, often online.
- *Expert:* Research involving public volunteers in data collection or classification (e.g., Zooniverse projects).

**Co-orbital**
- *Simple:* A small object that shares a planet's path around the Sun.
- *Expert:* A body in 1:1 mean-motion resonance with a planet: tadpole (Trojan), horseshoe or quasi-satellite configurations.

**Comet**
- *Simple:* An icy space object that grows a fuzzy cloud and tail when it comes near the Sun.
- *Expert:* A small body with volatile ices that sublimate near the Sun, producing a coma and tails.

## D

**Data**
- *Simple:* Information scientists collect, like pictures and measurements.
- *Expert:* Recorded observations with associated metadata and uncertainties.

**Dwarf planet**
- *Simple:* A round world that goes around the Sun but has not cleared other objects from its path, like Pluto.
- *Expert:* IAU 2006 definition: orbits the Sun, in hydrostatic equilibrium, has not cleared its orbital neighborhood, not a satellite.

## E

**Eccentricity (*e*)**
- *Simple:* A number that says how stretched an orbit is. 0 is a circle. Bigger numbers are more stretched. More than 1 means the path is open and the object escapes.
- *Expert:* Conic-section shape parameter: 0 circle, 0 < *e* < 1 ellipse, *e* = 1 parabola, *e* > 1 hyperbola.

**Ecliptic**
- *Simple:* The flat "road" in the sky that the Sun and planets seem to follow.
- *Expert:* The plane of Earth's orbit, used as a reference plane for Solar System orbital elements.

**Einstein ring / Einstein radius**
- *Simple:* A ring of light made when a heavy object lines up exactly in front of a faraway star.
- *Expert:* Angular radius θ_E = √[(4*GM*/*c*²) *D*ₗₛ/(*D*ₗ*D*ₛ)] of the image ring for perfect alignment.

**Ellipse**
- *Simple:* A stretched circle, the shape of most orbits.
- *Expert:* Closed conic with 0 ≤ *e* < 1; bound Keplerian orbit.

**Ephemeris (plural: ephemerides)**
- *Simple:* A table or computer model that says where things in space will be.
- *Expert:* Tabulated or computed positions of Solar System bodies, e.g., JPL DE series, INPOP.

**Escape speed**
- *Simple:* How fast something must go to get away from the Sun (or a planet) and never come back.
- *Expert:* *v*_esc = √(2*GM*/*r*); about 42.1 km/s relative to the Sun at 1 au.

**Evidence level**
- *Simple:* Our label for how sure scientists are: Confirmed, Strong evidence, Testable idea, or Long shot.
- *Expert:* Project-specific four-level scale; see the [style guide](../STYLE.md#4-evidence-levels).

**Exomoon**
- *Simple:* A moon around a planet in another solar system. None is confirmed yet.
- *Expert:* A natural satellite of an exoplanet; candidates (e.g., Kepler-1625 b-i, Kepler-1708 b-i) remain disputed.

**Exoplanet**
- *Simple:* A planet that goes around a star other than our Sun. Also called an "alien planet."
- *Expert:* An extrasolar planet; thousands are known, mostly from transit and radial-velocity surveys.

## F

**False positive**
- *Simple:* Something that looks like a discovery but is not.
- *Expert:* A detection that passes a test threshold but arises from noise, systematics or a different astrophysical source.

**Free-floating planet (rogue planet)**
- *Simple:* A planet that does not go around any star. It drifts through space alone.
- *Expert:* A planetary-mass object not gravitationally bound to a star; detected via microlensing or, when young and warm, direct imaging.

## G

**Gaia**
- *Simple:* A European space telescope that measured the positions and distances of over a billion stars.
- *Expert:* ESA astrometric mission providing parallaxes, proper motions and photometry; data releases DR1–DR3, with DR4 to follow.

**Gravity**
- *Simple:* The pull that every object with mass has on every other object.
- *Expert:* Described by Newton's law of universal gravitation in the weak-field limit, and by general relativity in full.

**Gravitational lensing**
- *Simple:* When gravity bends light, like a magnifying glass.
- *Expert:* Deflection of light by mass; strong, weak and micro-lensing regimes.

**GRAIL**
- *Simple:* Two NASA spacecraft that flew around the Moon in 2012 to map its gravity.
- *Expert:* Gravity Recovery and Interior Laboratory; measured inter-spacecraft range-rate with a Ka-band link to produce high-degree lunar gravity fields.

## H

**Hyperbola**
- *Simple:* An open, curved path. An object on this path passes the Sun once and leaves forever.
- *Expert:* Conic with *e* > 1; specific orbital energy ε > 0.

**Hyperbolic excess speed (*v*∞)**
- *Simple:* How fast a visitor from another star would be moving when it is still very far from the Sun.
- *Expert:* *v*∞ = √(2ε) = √[*GM*(*e* − 1)/*q*].

## I

**Impact parameter (*b*)**
- *Simple:* How close to the middle of a star a planet passes when it crosses in front.
- *Expert:* Sky-projected separation at conjunction in units of stellar radius; *b* = (*a*/*R*★) cos *i* for circular orbits.

**Inclination (*i*)**
- *Simple:* How tilted an orbit is.
- *Expert:* Angle between the orbital plane and a reference plane (the ecliptic for Solar System bodies; the sky plane for exoplanets).

**Infrared**
- *Simple:* A kind of light our eyes cannot see that warm things give off.
- *Expert:* Electromagnetic radiation with wavelengths roughly 0.7 μm to 1 mm.

**Interstellar object (ISO)**
- *Simple:* A comet or rock from another star system that passes through ours.
- *Expert:* A body on an unbound heliocentric orbit with *v*∞ > 0 inconsistent with Solar System origin; known: 1I, 2I, 3I.

**Isolated Tracklet File (ITF)**
- *Simple:* A big public list of sky sightings of moving objects that have not yet been matched to any known object.
- *Expert:* The Minor Planet Center's database of observations not yet linked to designated objects.

## K

**Kuiper Belt**
- *Simple:* A ring of icy objects beyond Neptune, including Pluto.
- *Expert:* The trans-Neptunian population roughly 30–50 au from the Sun, with classical, resonant and scattered components.

## L

**Lagrange points**
- *Simple:* Five special spots where a small object can move along with two bigger ones, like a puppy trotting beside its owner.
- *Expert:* Equilibrium points L1–L5 of the circular restricted three-body problem; L4/L5 linearly stable for mass ratio ≳ 25.

**Light curve**
- *Simple:* A graph of how bright something is over time.
- *Expert:* Time series of flux or magnitude.

**Light-year**
- *Simple:* How far light travels in one year — about 9.5 trillion km.
- *Expert:* 9.4607 × 10¹⁵ m; parsec ≈ 3.26 light-years.

**Look-elsewhere effect**
- *Simple:* If you look in enough places, you will find something odd just by chance.
- *Expert:* Inflation of false-alarm probability when many hypotheses are tested; corrected by trials factors or simulation.

## M

**Microlensing**
- *Simple:* When a star or planet passes in front of a faraway star and makes it look brighter for a while.
- *Expert:* Gravitational lensing with unresolved images; detected as transient magnification with characteristic timescale *t*_E.

**Minor Planet Center (MPC)**
- *Simple:* The official place where sightings of asteroids and comets are collected.
- *Expert:* IAU-sanctioned clearinghouse for small-body astrometry, designations and orbits.

**Monotransit (single transit)**
- *Simple:* A planet that was seen crossing its star only once.
- *Expert:* A single transit event in a data set, usually indicating a period longer than the observing baseline.

## N

**NEOWISE / WISE**
- *Simple:* A NASA space telescope that took infrared pictures of the whole sky many times.
- *Expert:* Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer (launched 2009) and its reactivated asteroid-hunting mission NEOWISE (2013–2024).

## O

**Obliquity (stellar)**
- *Simple:* How tilted a planet's path is compared with the way its star spins.
- *Expert:* Angle ψ between the stellar spin axis and the orbit normal; projected value λ measured via the Rossiter–McLaughlin effect.

**Oort Cloud**
- *Simple:* A huge, far-away cloud of icy objects surrounding the Solar System.
- *Expert:* Hypothesized roughly spherical reservoir of comets at ~10³–10⁵ au, source of long-period comets.

**Orbit**
- *Simple:* The path one object takes around another.
- *Expert:* Trajectory under gravitational influence; Keplerian in the two-body approximation.

## P

**Parallax**
- *Simple:* The way an object seems to shift when you look from two places. Close things shift more.
- *Expert:* Apparent angular displacement due to observer motion; distance *d* (pc) = 1/ϖ (arcsec).

**Parsec (pc)**
- *Simple:* A distance astronomers use, about 3.26 light-years.
- *Expert:* Distance at which 1 au subtends 1 arcsecond; ≈ 3.0857 × 10¹⁶ m.

**Polarization**
- *Simple:* When light waves wiggle mostly in one direction.
- *Expert:* Described by Stokes parameters (I, Q, U, V); produced by scattering, reflection, magnetic fields.

**Precession**
- *Simple:* A slow wobble or turning of a spinning or orbiting object's tilt.
- *Expert:* Secular rotation of an angular-momentum vector or orbital node/apse due to torques.

**Proper motion**
- *Simple:* How fast a star seems to drift across the sky over years.
- *Expert:* Angular rate of change of position (μ, usually in mas/yr or ″/yr).

## R

**Radial velocity**
- *Simple:* How fast something is moving toward or away from us.
- *Expert:* Line-of-sight velocity measured via Doppler shifts; used to detect planets.

**Resonance (orbital)**
- *Simple:* When two orbits "keep time" with each other, like 2 laps for every 1.
- *Expert:* Commensurability of orbital frequencies (mean-motion) or of precession frequencies (secular).

**Retrograde orbit**
- *Simple:* An orbit that goes the "wrong way," opposite to the planets.
- *Expert:* Inclination *i* > 90°.

**Rossiter–McLaughlin effect**
- *Simple:* A trick to tell if a planet's path lines up with its star's spin, by watching colors shift as the planet crosses.
- *Expert:* Anomalous radial-velocity signal during transit as the planet occults rotating stellar surface regions.

**Rubin Observatory**
- *Simple:* A new telescope in Chile that photographs the whole southern sky every few nights.
- *Expert:* NSF–DOE Vera C. Rubin Observatory, conducting the Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST).

## S

**Secular**
- *Simple:* Very slow, happening over thousands to millions of years.
- *Expert:* Long-term perturbations averaged over orbital periods.

**SETI**
- *Simple:* The Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence — looking for signs of technology from other worlds. None has been found.
- *Expert:* Searches for technosignatures, especially narrowband radio and optical pulses.

**Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)**
- *Simple:* How strong a signal is compared with the random "fuzz" in the data.
- *Expert:* Ratio of signal amplitude to noise standard deviation (or equivalent detection statistic).

**Survey**
- *Simple:* A telescope project that looks at large parts of the sky again and again.
- *Expert:* A systematic observing program with defined footprint, cadence and depth.

## T

**TESS (Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite)**
- *Simple:* A NASA space telescope that watches stars for the tiny dips made by planets.
- *Expert:* All-sky transit survey launched in 2018, observing ~27-day sectors.

**Technosignature**
- *Simple:* A sign of technology, like a radio beacon, that might show someone built it.
- *Expert:* Observable evidence of technology; a target of SETI searches.

**Tisserand parameter (*T*_J)**
- *Simple:* A number that helps sort orbits and tells us how an object's path relates to Jupiter's.
- *Expert:* *T*_J = *a*_J/*a* + 2 cos *i* √[(*a*/*a*_J)(1 − *e*²)]; approximately conserved in the CR3BP.

**Tracklet**
- *Simple:* A few sightings of the same moving dot on one night.
- *Expert:* A short set of detections (typically 2–4 within an hour or so) linked as a single moving object.

**Transit**
- *Simple:* When a planet passes in front of its star and blocks a little light.
- *Expert:* Primary eclipse of a star by a planet; depth ≈ (*R*ₚ/*R*★)².

**Transit timing / duration variations (TTV / TDV)**
- *Simple:* When a planet's crossing comes a little early or late, or takes a little more or less time, than expected.
- *Expert:* Deviations from a linear ephemeris (TTV) or constant duration (TDV), caused by perturbers, moons or orbital evolution.

**Trojan**
- *Simple:* A small object that shares a planet's orbit, about 60° ahead or behind.
- *Expert:* Co-orbital librating about L4 or L5.

## U

**Uncertainty (error bar)**
- *Simple:* How much a measurement could be off.
- *Expert:* Quantified dispersion of an estimate, e.g., standard deviation or credible interval.

## Y

**Y dwarf**
- *Simple:* The coldest kind of brown dwarf. The coldest one known is about as cold as a kitchen freezer.
- *Expert:* Spectral class of brown dwarfs with *T*_eff ≲ 500 K; WISE 0855−0714 (~250 K) is the coldest known.
