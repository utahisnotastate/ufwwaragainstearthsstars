# What is Future Disclosure: Astronomy?

> **In one sentence:** An open science project that points to places where tomorrow's discoveries may already be waiting in today's public astronomy data — and gives everyone, from kids to professionals, the tools to look.

## For young readers

Telescopes collect more pictures than people can ever look at closely. Some discoveries are probably sitting in those pictures right now, waiting for someone to notice. This project is a treasure map. It shows where to look, tells you how sure we are, and gives you tools to search. Anyone can help — even you!

## The name

- **Future** — these are discoveries we think are *likely to be made*, not ones already made.
- **Disclosure** — we openly share each idea, our evidence, and how it could be wrong. Nothing here is secret, and we do not claim that anyone is hiding anything.
- **Alien planets** — planets around other stars (exoplanets). This project makes **no claims about alien life or alien technology**.

## Our principles

1. **Honest evidence levels.** Every claim is labeled: Confirmed, Strong evidence, Testable idea, or Long shot.
2. **Respect for scientists.** Unexamined data is a normal part of science, not a scandal.
3. **Show the way to be wrong.** Every idea comes with a test that could disprove it.
4. **Simple first, detailed second.** Every page starts with a part a child can read.
5. **Open everything.** Code under the MIT license; writing under CC BY 4.0.
6. **Safety.** No health advice. Child-safe participation. Never look at the Sun.

## Independence

This is an independent community project. It is **not** affiliated with or endorsed by NASA, ESA, the Minor Planet Center, the SETI Institute, or any other organization named in these pages. We name them because their public data and published work make this project possible.

## Where it came from

The project began as private brainstorming notes. We kept ideas that can be tested with accepted physics and public data, rewrote them for clarity, corrected errors, and removed the rest. See [editorial notes](editorial-notes.md).
