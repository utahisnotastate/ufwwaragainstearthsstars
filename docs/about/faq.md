# Frequently Asked Questions

> **In one sentence:** Short, honest answers to the questions people ask most about this project.

## For young readers

These are questions people often ask. The first answer in each one is short and simple. If you want more, keep reading.

---

### Is this about aliens?

**No.** "Alien planets" means planets around other stars. Scientists call them **exoplanets**. We have not found aliens, and this project does not claim to.

*More detail:* Radio telescopes have been pointed at all three known interstellar objects (ʻOumuamua, Borisov and 3I/ATLAS) to look for signs of technology. None was found. See the [guide for SETI researchers](../audiences/seti-researchers.md).

### Is anyone hiding planets?

**No.** Space data is huge, and scientists have limited time. Some signals wait for someone to study them. That is normal, not secret.

*More detail:* Most of the data we use is public on purpose: missions like TESS, Kepler, WISE and GRAIL make their data free for everyone. Scientists must also avoid false alarms, which takes careful work. The word "disclosure" in our name means openly sharing where discoveries **might** be found. It does not mean anything was hidden.

### What is a "disclosure" here?

**A short report about one place where a discovery might be waiting in public data.** Each one says what might be there, how sure we are, and how anyone could check.

See the [disclosure registry](../disclosures/README.md).

### What do the colored levels mean?

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🟢 Level 1 — Confirmed | Seen and checked more than once. |
| 🔵 Level 2 — Strong evidence | Good published results, still being discussed. |
| 🟡 Level 3 — Testable idea | Follows normal physics, not shown yet; you can help test it. |
| 🟠 Level 4 — Long shot | Physics allows it, but today's tools may be too weak. |

### Can I really discover something?

**Maybe — and you can definitely help.** People without science degrees have found real comets, asteroids, brown dwarfs and planets.

*More detail:* Gennady Borisov, an amateur astronomer, discovered the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov in 2019 with a telescope he built. Volunteers on Zooniverse projects such as **Backyard Worlds: Planet 9** and **Planet Hunters TESS** have helped find brown dwarfs and planets. Most possible discoveries turn out to be noise or known objects, so careful checking matters. Read [how to report a discovery](how-to-report-a-discovery.md) before announcing anything.

### Why were some ideas removed?

**Because they disagree with physics that has been tested very carefully, or because they made health claims without good evidence.**

*More detail:* This project grew from brainstorming notes. We kept every idea that can be tested with accepted physics and public data, and rewrote it in plain language with real references. We removed ideas that need unproven physics (for example, faster-than-light "true position" sensors or "scalar" energy fields) and all health and "frequency healing" advice. The full list, with reasons, is in the [editorial notes](editorial-notes.md).

### Is this endorsed by NASA?

**No.** This is an independent project. We use public data from NASA, ESA and other organizations, and we thank them, but they do not endorse us.

*More detail:* We do not use agency logos. See the [guide for space agency professionals](../audiences/space-agency-professionals.md).

### Who runs this project?

**Volunteers.** Anyone can suggest changes. Decisions and corrections are made in public in the repository.

### Is the science checked?

**We try hard, and we fix mistakes in public.** Every page must cite real sources. If you find an error, please open an issue.

*More detail:* Our [style guide](../STYLE.md) requires checking every number and name against a primary source and never inventing references.

### Can teachers use this in class?

**Yes!** Start with the [lessons](../lessons/README.md) and the [guide for teachers and tutors](../audiences/teachers-and-tutors.md).

### What ages is this for?

**Everyone from about age 6 up.** Each page starts with a simple part and ends with an expert part. The [kids' page](../audiences/kids.md) is a good start for children.

### Did a planet really disappear in 2026?

**The planet did not disappear — its shadow did.** TESS watched the giant planet TOI-2535 b pass in front of its star from 2019 to 2026. Its dips got smaller and then stopped. Scientists think an unseen neighbor planet is slowly tilting its orbit, so it no longer crosses in front of the star from our point of view.

*More detail:* Yee et al. (2026), *The Astrophysical Journal Letters*. See [D02](../disclosures/d02-vanishing-transits.md).

### Is there a "Planet Nine"?

**We don't know yet.** Some scientists think an unseen planet far beyond Neptune could explain how some distant icy objects move. Others think the pattern may come from where telescopes happened to look. No such planet has been found as of November 2026.

*More detail:* See [D01](../disclosures/d01-unseen-planets-far-solar-system.md). It is labeled 🟡 Level 3 — Testable idea.

### Can a backyard telescope find planets around other stars?

**It can watch known planets cross their stars, which helps scientists.** Finding brand-new exoplanets with a small telescope is very hard. Some claimed backyard methods (such as detecting planets by polarized light) need far bigger telescopes.

*More detail:* See the [guide for amateur astronomers](../audiences/amateur-astronomers.md).

### Does this project give health or wellness advice?

**No.** Health and "frequency" claims from the early notes were removed because there is no good evidence for them. For health questions, talk to a qualified medical professional.
